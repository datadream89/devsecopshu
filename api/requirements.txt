Task: Redesign Slide 3 of the ClaimTrack iTournament 2026 presentation

Act as a senior healthcare claims operations expert, executive presentation designer, and innovation pitch strategist.

Redesign Slide 3 only, using the existing PowerPoint presentation, its design language, and any available internal workflow documentation or data.

The objective is to produce an exceptional, executive-ready slide that is visually compelling, operationally accurate, and understandable within 25–35 seconds.

Do not redesign Slides 1 or 2. Do not modify any other slide.

1. Narrative context

Slide 1 introduces ClaimTrack through the familiar experience of tracking a delivery.

Slide 2 shows a de-identified myCigna COB-pended claim with the headline:

“Members are told what's wrong, but not what happens next.”

It raises three questions:

1. Who handles my claim after I respond?
2. Did you receive what I sent?
3. What happens next—and when?

Slide 3 must answer the audience's natural follow-up:

What actually happens after the member submits the requested COB information?

Slide 4 will quantify pend timing and outcomes. Do not introduce Slide 4's statistics here.

2. Slide 3 headline

Use:

“The member responds. But the claim can remain pending.”

Make the headline prominent, dark navy, and consistent with Slide 2.

Optional short subtitle:

“Submitting information is only the beginning of the resolution process.”

3. Replace the existing four-party diagram with a resolution workflow

The existing Slide 3 depicts Member, Provider, Claims Operations, and Claims Platform.

Replace this generic participant diagram with a simplified, accurate COB resolution journey.

Design the main visual around three stages:

Stage 1 — MEMBER RESPONDS

The member provides the requested Coordination of Benefits information.

Use a simple member/document icon.

Stage 2 — INFORMATION REVIEW

The submitted information must be:

- Located and associated with the relevant claim or outstanding request.
- Validated against the specific pend reason.
- Assessed for completeness.

Use a document-verification or claims-review icon.

Do not invent specific departments, assignment mechanisms, processing times, or automation capabilities.

Stage 3 — TWO POSSIBLE OUTCOMES

Create a clear visual split into two branches.

COMPLETE — Processing resumes

- Outstanding correspondence is marked received.
- Claim and related claim history are reviewed.
- Claim processing resumes.
- Member-facing myCigna status transitions from Pending to Processing.

Use a restrained teal/green accent to indicate forward progress.

INCOMPLETE — Returns to pending

- Required information remains incomplete.
- The claim can return to an external pending state.
- Additional information or follow-up may be required.
- The member-facing myCigna experience may continue to show Pending without clearly identifying what remains missing.

Use a restrained amber/rust accent to distinguish this path.

Visually emphasize the repeat-pending loop.

Show a subtle curved arrow from the incomplete branch back toward the information-request or review stage.

Label it:

“Repeat-pending loop”

The loop must be understandable immediately without requiring a detailed verbal explanation.

4. Critical member-experience insight

The current myCigna experience already provides meaningful claim statuses, including Pending and Processing.

Do not claim that Cigna has no tracking or that members never receive updates.

Instead, distinguish:

- Claim status visibility.
- Visibility into information receipt, completeness, review progress, and the next required action.

The specific opportunity is that a member may submit information, yet the claim can remain Pending without the member-facing experience clearly explaining the outstanding gap.

Do not assert that Cigna never sends follow-up correspondence through other channels. That has not been established.

5. Bottom takeaway

Include one concise takeaway across the bottom of the slide:

“A member can respond without knowing whether the information was sufficient—or what happens next.”

Give this statement visual prominence using the presentation's existing restrained accent style.

Avoid excessive warning symbols, decorative graphics, or dense explanatory paragraphs.

6. Visual design requirements

Preserve the established ClaimTrack visual identity:

- White background.
- Dark navy headlines.
- Aqua/teal accents for progress.
- Rust/amber accents for unresolved information.
- Clean corporate typography.
- Consistent top navigation: Problem | Solution | Value | Pilot | Ask.
- Keep Problem highlighted.
- Preserve the existing corporate footer and slide number 3.
- Maintain generous whitespace, balanced alignment, and consistent iconography.

Use native editable PowerPoint shapes, connectors, icons, and text wherever possible.

Do not turn the slide into a static image.

Prefer a horizontal workflow with a clearly differentiated two-branch outcome on the right, provided all labels remain legible in presentation mode.

Use no more than three main stages. Keep detailed operational wording in speaker notes rather than overcrowding the slide.

7. Operational and data validation

Use available internal documentation or datasets only where access is authorized.

If internal documentation is available, validate the workflow against it, including the conditions for marking correspondence received and resuming claim processing.

If verified data is available about repeat pends or incomplete COB responses, evaluate whether one concise supporting statistic would materially improve the slide.

Only include such a statistic if its definition, denominator, date range, and population are confirmed.

Otherwise, keep Slide 3 entirely process-focused.

Do not invent numbers, operational rules, notifications, service levels, or system capabilities.

Do not include day-55 or North Carolina day-90 limits without verifying their applicability.

8. Presentation and storytelling

The slide should create a clear realization:

Responding to a request does not guarantee that the pend has been resolved.

The complete branch should feel straightforward.

The incomplete branch should reveal the potential repeat-pending cycle and member uncertainty.

Do not portray all COB claims as following the incomplete branch.

Do not imply that incomplete submissions have been proven to cause the deadline-related outcomes presented on Slide 4.

The slide must connect naturally to Slide 2 and prepare the audience for Slide 4's quantitative evidence.

9. Speaker notes

Add this concise narration to Slide 3 speaker notes:

“Once a member responds, the information must be located and validated against the reason the claim was pended.

If it's complete, the correspondence is marked received, the claim and its history are reviewed, and processing resumes.

But if something is still missing, the claim can return to pending.

And here's the challenge: the member may still see Pending without clearly knowing what information remains outstanding.

So responding doesn't necessarily mean the claim is moving toward resolution.”

End with this transition to Slide 4:

“That's one way uncertainty can persist. Now let's look at what the broader pend data tells us.”

Before completing the modification, verify that:

- The repeat-pending loop is unmistakable.
- The slide does not overstate limitations of existing Cigna systems.
- All operational claims are supportable.
- Text is readable on a projected screen.
- The design is consistent with Slides 1 and 2.
- All elements remain editable.
- No other slides have been modified.

Deliver the finished Slide 3, not merely recommendations. Prioritize operational credibility, clarity, and visual storytelling over decorative complexity.
